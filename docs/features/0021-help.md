# 0021 — Help

**Status:** approved 2026-11-18
**Milestone:** M4
**Drafted:** 2026-11-18

## Goal

The player can call up the help with one key: a small box over the game that lists every key
and what it does. The game stands still while the box is open. For the first ten seconds after
the game starts, one line at the bottom of the screen names that key.

The feature comes from the walk of 0019 on 2026-11-18. Its feel note on the key: `L` is right,
but the game as a whole needs a small help menu that can be called up, and a hint to it after
the game starts.

## Scope

Built on 0020 as merged: it uses `GameState.held` and the press of 0020, and it lists `J`. No
tile changes, no map changes, no new sprite, no new colour.

**In:**

- The action `help`, on `H`, and its press. The game now has eight actions.
- `src/game/help.ts`: `GameState.help`, opening and closing, and when the start hint is due.
- While the help is open, nothing is stepped — not the world, not an open dialogue, not the
  open journal.
- `src/render/draw-help.ts`: the seven rows and the box. The scene draws it over whatever is
  on screen.
- The start hint: the line `notice.help` in the notice's bar for the first 600 steps, where no
  other text is on screen.
- The language table gets fifteen keys in German and English.
- With the implementation: `gameplay.md` §7 and §8; `vision.md` §2; `architecture.md` §1, §2,
  §4, §5 and §8; `visual.md` §3; `testing.md` §1 and §2; the README.
- With the approval: `docs/plan.md` §4 names 0021 as the sixth feature of M4. The content
  budget of `docs/plan.md` §3 is not touched.

**Out, explicitly:** any text that explains the game — what the shadow is, what the lamp is
for, what to do next; that is not deferred, the game has none (`vision.md` §2) · the title
screen and what it shows of the keys (M8) · a hint that comes back — after a time without a
key, after the dark, for the next visitor at a booth; that is decided with the booth set-up,
before the first trade fair, or with the title screen (M8) · remembering over a reload that
the help was seen — storing anything in the browser is decided with the save states (M8) · the
journal's own keys beyond `J`: the arrows that turn its tabs are not listed; a line on the
journal's screen is the journal's matter (a feel question of 0020) · remappable keys and a row
for the gamepad (`docs/plan.md` §5) · a test that ties the rows to the binding table — the
table lives in `main.ts`, which no unit test loads; the rows are held to this spec's table
instead · opening the help with a click · the help in the dark · pages or scrolling in the
box · sound.

## Behaviour

Refines `gameplay.md` §7 and §8, `vision.md` §2, `architecture.md` §2, §4, §5 and §8 and
`visual.md` §3.

### What this settles in the specification

Three sentences that exist today meet this feature. Each is settled here in the open, with the
words the specification gets.

1. **`gameplay.md` §7 calls the journal "the only screen besides the game itself".** That
   stays true. The help is not a screen: the scene does not draw it instead of the world, as it
   draws the journal, but as a box over the world, as it draws the dialogue box. The floor
   stays visible on all four sides of it. §7 gets one sentence so that nobody reads the help as
   a second screen: *"The help (feature 0021) is no second screen: it is a box over whatever
   is on screen, as the dialogue box is."*
2. **`vision.md` §2 says the booth teaser "has to be readable without a word of tutorial
   text".** That stays true as well, and §2 gets the sentences that say what tutorial text is:
   *"Tutorial text is text that explains the game: what the shadow is, what the lamp is for,
   what to do next. The game has none. The help (feature 0021) is not tutorial text: a list of
   keys the player calls up, and one line at the start that names its key. It says which key
   does what, and nothing about why."* The reasons: the help is called up and never put in
   front of the player; the hint is one line, in the bar every notice uses, and leaves on its
   own; and where the two purposes of §2 pull apart, the first wins — a player at home has
   nobody beside them who knows that `Space` holds the lamp.
3. **0019 and 0020 both put "a hint on screen that names the key" out of scope.** This feature
   is where it lands: the rows name every key, and the start hint names the key of the help.
   The two feature files stay as they are; they are history.

### The action and the state

The input table of `main.ts` gets `help` ← `KeyH`, and the game's input the boolean `help`. The
game now has eight actions. `H` is the first letter of the word in both languages.

`GameState.help` is a boolean, `false` at the start. It is in the state, like the dialogue and
the journal and unlike the language: the help stops the game, so it is a fact a step has to
know, and the rule that nothing moves can be tested without a browser. `help` is **pressed** in
a step when the input holds it and `held` does not (0020).

### Opening and closing

In a step that is not a step of the dark, in this order:

1. **The help is open.** `tick` grows by 1 and `held` is set; nothing else is stepped — not
   the player, the lamp, the creatures, the movers, the walls or a figure, not the page of an
   open dialogue and not the tab of the open journal. When `help` is pressed, `help` becomes
   `false`. The other keys do nothing.
2. **The help is not open, and `help` is pressed.** `help` becomes `true`, whatever else is
   open and whatever else the input holds. `tick` grows by 1, `held` is set, and nothing else
   is stepped in this step.
3. **Otherwise** the step is the one of 0020.

In the dark `help` does nothing. The help has no memory: it opens as the same box every time.

The help lies over what is there. With a dialogue open it opens, the dialogue stays on its
page, and it is still on that page when the help closes. With the journal open it opens, the
journal stays on its tab, and it is still on that tab when the help closes. `help` and
`journal` pressed in the same step open the help and not the journal.

Nothing else in the state changes when the help opens: a figure in mid-step, a push that is
running, a creature in the light and a lamp that is on stay as they are and go on when it
closes. Because every step of the help sets `held`, a key held while the help closes is not
pressed: a held `talk` turns no page, a held `right` turns no tab, and a held direction key
walks the figure from the next ordinary step.

### The start hint

`HELP_HINT_STEPS` is 600, ten seconds, *tune*. The **start hint** is the key `notice.help`,
drawn like the notice of 0016: the bar (8, 158, 304, 18) in `#181425` with the line centred in
it. It is drawn when all of these hold:

1. `tick` < `HELP_HINT_STEPS`;
2. the help is not open, and the journal is not open;
3. no dialogue is open;
4. the notice of 0016 does not show;
5. the language line of 0019 is not due.

So the hint has the last place in the bar: the dialogue box comes first, then the notice, then
the language line, then the hint. A switch of the language within the first ten seconds shows
its line for two seconds, and then the hint is back, in the new language. The hint is derived
from `tick` in every frame; the state has no field for it. `tick` grows in every step, also
while the help, a dialogue or the journal is open, so the ten seconds run on behind them. The
hint does not come back: not after the dark, and not after a time without a key.

### The text

| Key | `de` | `en` |
|---|---|---|
| `notice.help` | `Hilfe: Taste H` | `Help: press H` |
| `help.key-walk` | `Pfeiltasten, WASD` | `Arrow keys, WASD` |
| `help.do-walk` | `Gehen` | `Walk` |
| `help.key-lamp` | `Leertaste` | `Space` |
| `help.do-lamp` | `Lampe halten` | `Hold the lamp` |
| `help.key-talk` | `E, Enter` | `E, Enter` |
| `help.do-talk` | `Sprechen, weiter` | `Talk, next page` |
| `help.key-journal` | `J` | `J` |
| `help.do-journal` | `Journal` | `Journal` |
| `help.key-language` | `L` | `L` |
| `help.do-language` | `Sprache wechseln` | `Switch language` |
| `help.key-fullscreen` | `F` | `F` |
| `help.do-fullscreen` | `Vollbild` | `Fullscreen` |
| `help.key-help` | `H` | `H` |
| `help.do-help` | `Hilfe schließen` | `Close help` |

The rules, held by a test in both languages:

- `notice.help` is one line, and the notice bar holds it;
- a key of the form `help.key-…` is one line of at most 17 characters;
- a key of the form `help.do-…` is one line of at most 16 characters.

A row says which key and what it does, in as few words as a label has. No row says why. The
letter of a key is a string of the table like every other text (INV-4), also where it is the
same in both languages.

`src/render/draw-help.ts` holds `HELP_ROWS`, the seven rows in this order, each a pair of keys:
walk, lamp, talk, journal, language, fullscreen, help. The rows are what the box shows and no
rule of the game reads them, so they live with the drawing.

### Drawing

While `GameState.help` is `true`, the scene draws what it would draw otherwise — the world with
its hearts and the dialogue box or the notice, or the journal — but neither the language line
nor the start hint, and then the help over it. The lids of the dark never meet the help: it
does not open in the dark, and no dark begins while it is open.

`src/render/draw-help.ts` draws, in the language of the scene's settings (0019), on the surface
and not shifted by the camera:

1. the frame: the rectangle (48, 30, 224, 98) in `#3a4466`;
2. the field: the rectangle (49, 31, 222, 96) in `#181425`, so that 1 px of the frame is left
   on every side;
3. row k, counted from 0: the key from (56, 38 + 12k) and what it does from (170, 38 + 12k).

A key of 17 characters ends at x = 158, and 12 px lie between the columns. A second column of
16 characters ends at x = 266. The last row ends at y = 120, 7 px above the field's lower edge,
as the first begins 7 px below its upper edge.

The box keeps clear of everything else that is drawn on the surface: the hearts end at y = 12,
the journal's tabs and their rule at y = 24, and the dialogue box begins at y = 134. So the
dialogue box with its page and the journal's tabs stay readable under an open help. The frame
is what makes the box a box on the journal's screen, which has the field's colour.

When the language is switched while the help is open, the rows are drawn in the new language
from the next frame; the box is the text that answers the switch, and no language line is
drawn.

### Edge cases

- **A held key.** `H` held down opens the help once; it does not close it again, and it does
  not flicker.
- **The tab loses focus.** Every key is released (`architecture.md` §4) and no step runs while
  the page is hidden. The help is still open when the player comes back, and `H` closes it.
- **Before the content has loaded** there is no loop, and the key does nothing.
- **`H` with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd.** `main.ts` leaves the browser its shortcut, as for every bound
  key. The keyboard does not look at modifiers, so the game sees the key as it sees `J` with
  Ctrl; this feature does not change that.
- **Fullscreen.** `F` works while the help is open, as it works everywhere; it never reaches
  the game.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The initial state has `help` `false`, and survives a JSON round trip at the start and with
   the help open.
2. A press of `help` outside the dark opens the help: with nothing else open, also while
   `right`, `lamp` and `talk` are held and during a push; with a dialogue open, which keeps
   its page; and with the journal open on `cases`, which keeps its tab.
3. With the help open, 600 steps with every key but `help` held change nothing but `tick` and
   `held`: the player, the lamp, the creatures, the movers, the walls, the figures, the
   dialogue, the journal, overview and `dark` are as they were.
4. A press of `help` closes the help, and the next step is the one of 0020. A key held for 600
   steps opens the help once and does not close it.
5. When the help closes over a dialogue with `talk` held since before, the page is the same
   one step later; when it closes over the journal with `right` held since before, the tab is
   the same one step later.
6. In the dark `help` does nothing, and `held` is set.
7. `help` and `journal` pressed in the same step leave `help` `true` and `journal` `null`.
8. `de` and `en` hold the fifteen keys of the spec, and every one obeys the rule of its kind.
   `HELP_ROWS` has the seven rows in the order of the spec.
9. With nothing else on screen, the scene draws the bar and `notice.help` at `tick` 0 and at
   `tick` 599 — in German from (118, 162), in English from (121, 162) — and neither at `tick`
   600.
10. At `tick` 100 the scene draws no start hint with a dialogue open, with the notice of 0016
    showing, with the language line due, with the journal open or with the help open. With
    the notice showing it draws the notice, and with the language line due the language line.
11. With the help open over the world, the scene draws everything it draws without it, then
    the frame, the field, and the seven rows — the key of row 0 from (56, 38), what it does
    from (170, 38), those of row 6 from (56, 110) and (170, 110) — and nothing after them.
12. With the help open, the scene draws no language line at any `tick`. With the settings'
    language `en` it draws the English rows.
13. With the help open over a dialogue, the scene draws the dialogue box and its page and then
    the help; over the journal, the journal and then the help.
14. A state in which `help` is never held steps exactly as before this feature: the player,
    the creatures, the movers, the walls, the dialogue and the journal of 600 recorded steps
    are the same.

## Open questions

None. Seven were settled on 2026-11-18, each as drafted:

- The key: `H` opens and closes the help. Not `F1`, which browsers and systems keep for their
  own help; not `Escape`, on which the browser leaves fullscreen; not `?`, which sits on a
  different key in every layout.
- What it lists: seven rows, one per thing the player can do — walk, lamp, talk, journal,
  language, fullscreen, and the help itself — and no title. The arrows that turn the
  journal's tabs are not listed, and nothing explains the game.
- The pause: the game stands still while the help is open, so `help` is in the state. The
  help also opens over a dialogue and over the journal, and not in the dark.
- The start hint: `notice.help` in the notice's bar for the first ten seconds after the load,
  behind every other text, and it does not come back. A hint that returns after a time
  without a key is the booth's case and is left for later.
- Screen or box: a box over the game, 224x98, so `gameplay.md` §7 keeps its sentence about
  the only screen and gets the one sentence of this spec.
- The milestone: M4, as its sixth feature after 0020. When M8 brings the title screen, it
  decides whether the start hint stays or moves there; the help itself stays.
- The words: the fifteen strings of the table under "The text", in German and English —
  among them `Journal` as the German word for the journal, and `Enter` for the key in both
  languages.
