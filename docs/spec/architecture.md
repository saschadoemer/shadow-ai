# Architecture — Shadow AI

How the code is cut, what the state looks like, how a frame happens, and how a map gets from
Tiled into the game. The decision behind all of it is ADR-0001: an own minimal engine on
TypeScript and Canvas 2D, no framework.

What may import what: `main.ts` imports everything. `render/` imports `engine/`, the types and
constants of `game/`, and the keys and the lookup of `text/`. `game/` imports the math, constants and types of `engine/` and the keys of
`text/`, nothing else. `engine/` imports nothing from the project. `tests/arch/` enforces the two
arrows that matter: nothing in `game/` reaches `render/` or the DOM.

`window.shadowAi.state` is the state `main.ts` passed to the last render. It exists in every
build, because the smoke test reads the player's position from it against the dev server and
against the container alike. Beside it, `window.shadowAi.language` is the language of the last
render (feature 0019). It is read-only by convention; nothing in the game reads it, and
`game/` does not know it (INV-2).

## 2. The state model

One `GameState` object holds everything that changes while playing. Today that is `tick`, the
logic steps since the start, the player, the camera, the lamp, the upgrades, the creatures, the movers, the
standing shadow walls, the traces, the solved cases, the step that closed the close, the
standing figures, the open dialogue,
the open journal,
whether the help is open, the input of the last step, the captured count, overview and the step
of the dark; later the current map id
and cleared regions join them. It is plain data: numbers,
strings, booleans, arrays and plain objects. No functions, no class instances, no `Map` or `Set`,
no `Date`. A JSON round trip reproduces it exactly, and a test proves that on a populated state.

The player is `{ x, y, facing, frame, frameTimer, grace, recoil }`. `x` and `y` are the feet position in map
pixels, floating point: at 80 px/s a step moves 1.333 px, and the position is rounded only for
drawing (§5). `facing` is one of `'down'`, `'up'`, `'left'`, `'right'`. `frame` is the phase,
0–4: 0 is standing, 1–4 are the walk cycle. `frameTimer` counts the steps spent in the current
phase; when it reaches 7.5 the phase advances and 7.5 is subtracted, so the timer is always a
multiple of 0.5, which floating point holds exactly. `grace` counts the steps the figure is
still safe from a hit, 0–90; `recoil` is `{ dx, dy, steps }`, the push per step in pixels and
the steps it still lasts, all 0 when there is none (feature 0009). `createInitialState(map)` builds the state
before the first step from the map: `tick` 0, the player at the map's `spawn`, facing down,
phase 0, timer 0, `grace` 0, no push. How the player moves, turns and stays inside the map is specified in feature
0004 (`gameplay.md` §8). The player's collision box is the lowest 8 px of the sprite, 12 px wide,
centred under the feet: `[x − 6, x + 6) × [y − 8, y)`. It is derived from the feet when a step
needs it and never stored. A step moves the box on X, then on Y, and snaps each axis flush to the
solid boxes it would enter (`engine/collision`, features 0005 and 0010); then it clamps the feet
to the map. Intervals are half-open, so touching a box is not overlapping it and a box flush
against a wall slides along it. The solid boxes are the union of the three tile layers (§6);
cells outside the map are open, and the clamp keeps the figure on it. The displacement is below
8 px per call, the smaller side of the figure's box: by less, a box cannot be carried across a
strip. The thinnest box is the 2 px partition (feature 0011); the test is not swept.

`camera` is the view's top-left corner in map pixels, two integers. `followPlayer` (`game/camera`,
feature 0006) centres the 320x180 view on the collision box's centre, (x, y − 4), clamps it to
[0, mapWidth − 320] × [0, mapHeight − 180] and rounds (`engine/camera`, `cameraFor`): clamp
first, then round. It is derived from the player after every step and from the spawn at the
start, and kept in the state so a saved game comes back as it was left.
`createInitialState(map)` lives in `game/update` beside `update`, so that `game/state` holds
types and constants only.

There are two starts (feature 0026). `createInitialState(map)` is a game **past its opening**:
no dialogue open, the lamp used. The tests of the earlier features step
from it. `newGame(map)` is what a player gets, and what `main.ts` starts with: the same state
with `dialogue` `{ speaker: 'story', index: 0, page: 0 }` — the call on its first page —,
`lamp` `{ on: false, strength: 1, used: false }` — the lamp not yet used — and, since feature 0036,
`presented` `null`: the card is shown.

`presented` is the tick of the step in which the card ended, or `null` while it is shown
(feature 0036, `game/card`); a game past its opening has 0. While it is `null` — `cardShown`
— a step is `stepCard` and nothing else, before the dark, the help and everything after them:
`tick` grows by one, the input is remembered in `held`, and nothing of the world is stepped.
The card ends in the step in which `talk` is pressed — `pressed` against `held`, so a key held
from before does not count — or in the step whose tick reaches `CARD_STEPS`, 180: that step
sets `presented` to its tick and changes nothing else. The call is open under the card from
the start, so the next step is the dialogue's, and the key that ended the card, still held, is
no press for it. The wake keeps `presented`; nothing sets it back to `null`.

The state does not record when the opening ended. Feature 0026 kept that as `started`, for the
start hint of the help alone; feature 0031 took the hint and the field away.

`lamp` is `{ on, strength, used }` (features 0007, 0014, 0026, 0039): `used` says whether the
lamp was on in any step of the game — `false` in a new game, `true` from the first step in
which `on` is `true`, and never `false` again. The player has the lamp from the first step:
feature 0039 took the field `owned` out of the state, and with it every rule that asked for
it. The wake keeps `used`. `on` is true in every step whose input holds `lamp`,
derived from the input and kept in the state so the renderer knows what to draw; `strength` is
`BASE_STRENGTH`, 1, from `createInitialState` on, and no step changes it before the upgrades of
M6 — the wake keeps it. A later field, charge (M6), joins them. While it is on, `stepPlayer` keeps the feet where they are, resets
the walk cycle to standing and turns the facing by the walking rule; no collision or clamp runs.
The cone is `game/lamp`: the apex 12 px above the feet, the facing's unit vector, 48 px long and
half-open, opening with slope 1/2 to each side; `inCone(point, cone)` is the one test the game
makes on it, and `FRAME_APEX` names where the apex sits in a 48x48 frame of the lamp sheet, per
facing.

`upgrades` is an array of the ids of the upgrades the player has earned, in the order they
were earned (feature 0029); the ids are those of `UPGRADES` in `game/world`. It is empty at the
start, and the wake keeps it. The step that solves a case also earns the upgrade of its region
(`earn` in `game/cases`): when `UPGRADES` has an upgrade that is earned in the region and
`upgrades` does not hold it, its id is appended and `lamp.strength` becomes
`strengthAfter(upgrades.length)`, the base strength plus the number of upgrades. No other step
changes either. Solving Sales earns nothing; solving Marketing earns `wide-cone` and a strength
of 2. The cone's **spread** is its slope to each side and is derived, not stored
(`spreadOf(upgrades)` in `game/lamp`): `SPREAD`, 1/2, without `wide-cone` and `WIDE_SPREAD`,
3/4, with it. `update` derives it once per ordinary step and hands it to the step of the
creatures, the walls and the traces; `inCone(point, cone, spread)` and
`meetsCone(box, cone, spread)` take it as their last argument, and without it they test the
narrow cone. A state whose `upgrades` is empty steps exactly as before 0029.

`creatures` is an array of `{ species, x, y, light, home }` in the order of the map's points
(feature 0008). `home` is the point the creature was made from; it never changes (feature 0009). `x` and `y` are the feet in map pixels, floating point, the bottom centre of a 16x16
sprite; the collision box is the figure's, `[x − 6, x + 6) × [y − 8, y)`; the body point is
(x, y − 8). `light` counts the steps the creature has been lit without a break, 0–59. `captured`
is one whole number per species. `update` steps the player, then each creature against the
stepped player (`game/creature`). A creature is lit when the lamp is on, its body point is in the
cone, and no opaque box lies on the line from the figure's box centre (x, y − 4) to the body
point (`engine/collision`, `sightBlocked`, given `map.opaque`, §6); low furniture lets the light
pass but still stops the walk (feature 0011). Lit, it stands and `light` grows by 1; at 60 it
leaves the array and its species' count grows by 1. Not lit, `light` becomes 0 and it moves
toward the figure's feet when they are closer than 64 px and farther than 8 px — by 2/3 px, or by
what is left to 8 px — through `moveBox`, then its feet are clamped so the sprite stays on the
map. `createInitialState` makes the creatures from `map.points` and rejects a name that is no
species.

Each species has its **habits** (feature 0032, `HABITS` in `game/creature`): the radius at
which it notices the figure, the distance of one step, and whether it is **shy** — whether it
moves only while it is not looked at. The snippet: 64 px, 2/3 px, not shy. The mirage: 96 px
(`MIRAGE_NOTICE_RADIUS`), 1 px, shy. A creature is **looked at** (`isWatched`) when the vector
from the figure's box centre (x, y − 4) to its body point has a component above 0 along the
figure's facing, and no opaque box lies on that line — the line of the light, against the same
map, the one with the walls standing. A shy creature that is looked at and not lit stands, with
`light` 0; otherwise it moves by the rule above with its own numbers. `SPECIES` is
`['snippet', 'mirage']`, so `captured` has two counts. Whether a creature is looked at is
derived in every step and every frame and is not in the state.

`overview` is `{ current, max }`, 5 of 5 at the start (feature 0012); `dark` is 0 while the game runs (feature
0009). The order of a step in `update`: the lamp is on when the input holds it and the figure is
not being pushed; the figure is stepped — `grace` drops by 1, not below 0, and a pushed figure
moves by its push through `moveBox` and the clamp, 3 px per step for 8 steps, without reading
the keys, keeping its facing, standing; the creatures are stepped; then the first free creature,
in array order, within 10 px of the feet of a figure whose `grace` is 0 hits (`game/overview`):
one point less, `grace` 90 and a push straight away from the creature — or, with the last point,
`dark` 1, the figure left as it is. A frozen creature never hits. One step holds at most one hit.
With `dark` above 0 a step is `stepDark` and nothing else: `dark` and `tick` grow by 1, the lamp
is off, the input is not read; in the step `dark` becomes 45 the figure stands on the wake point
facing down, standing, not safe and not pushed, overview is full and every creature still in the
array is at its home with `light` 0, `captured` unchanged, the camera on the figure; in the step
it would become 90 it is 0. `rested` is `null`, or `{ at, result }` (feature 0027, `game/rest`):
the last rest the player took — `at` the `tick` of the state that step gave, `result` one of
`REST_RESULTS`, `restored`, `full` or `shadow`. It is `null` at the start, the wake keeps it,
and the notice of a rest is derived from it (§8). The wake point is the map's point named `wake`, the spawn when there
is none; two stop the game at the start (`createInitialState`).

`movers` is an array of `{ kind, look, route, x, y, facing, frame, frameTimer, target, wait }`,
one per route in route order (feature 0015, `game/mover`). `kind` is `person`, `dog`, `vacuum` or
`drone`; `look` picks the picture — a person's number among the persons, a dog's among the dogs
modulo 2, else 0. `route` is the index of the route; the route's points stay in the map and are
not in the state: `update` rebuilds the routes from `map.points` with `routesOf` in every step and
keeps them nowhere — no cache, no module-level map, no field on `TileMap` (INV-1). `x` and `y` are
the feet in map pixels, floating point; `target` is the index of the point the mover heads for;
`wait` the steps it still stands on its point. `createInitialState` builds the routes, checks them
(§6) and puts each mover on its point 1, facing down, `frame` 0, `target` 1, `wait` the pause of
point 1. `update` steps the movers last, each against the figure after the hit, in three rules:
**yield** — a person, a dog or a vacuum whose feet are closer than 20 px to the figure's stands,
`frame` and `frameTimer` 0, and nothing else changes; **wait** — otherwise a `wait` above 0
falls by 1, a person shows frame 0 and a dog frame 2 (it sits); **move** — otherwise the mover
goes toward its target by its speed per step (person 2/3 px, dog 1, vacuum 1/3, drone 1/2) or by
what is left, faces along the leg (a vacuum and a drone always face down), and on arrival stands
exactly on the point, takes its pause and turns to the next point, the first after the last. An
arrival slack of 10⁻⁶ px absorbs the error of summed thirds. While it moves a person's `frame`
runs 1–4 at 15 steps per phase and a dog's changes between 0 and 1 every 8 steps. In the dark no
mover is stepped (`stepDark` and the wake leave the array as it is). Nothing about a mover is
solid or opaque: the figure, the creatures and the light pass, and no creature sees one.

`shadowWalls` is an array of `{ wall, lit, light }`, one entry per shadow wall still standing,
in the map's order (feature 0014, `game/shadow`). `wall` is the index of the wall among
`wallsOf(map.rects)`; its box stays in the map and its density in the world data
(`game/world`). `lit` says whether the light was on it in the last step; `light` counts the steps
it has been giving way without a break, 0–59. A lifted wall leaves the array and never comes
back. As with the routes, `update` rebuilds the walls from `map.rects` in every step and keeps
them nowhere. `createInitialState` reads the walls — and so rejects a map whose rectangles are no
walls (§6) — and starts every wall standing, `{ wall: n, lit: false, light: 0 }`. In a step the
figure, the push and the creatures are stepped against the map **with** the walls standing at
the start of the step (`withWalls`: each standing wall's box added, as solid and as opaque, to
every cell it covers, on a copy of the grids); then every standing wall is stepped against the
stepped figure and the map **as loaded**, so a wall does not block its own light. A wall is in
the light when the lamp is on, its box meets the cone (`meetsCone`, closed form: in the cone's
coordinates, near = max(d0, 0), far = min(d1, 48), and the gap from 0 across the facing to the
box is at most far × spread — far / 2 for the narrow cone —, with near ≤ far and near < 48), and no opaque box lies on the line from
(x, y − 4) to the point of the wall's box nearest to it — along the wall the box ends at its last
pixel. Not in the light: `lit` false, `light` 0. In the light with `lamp.strength` below the
wall's density: `lit` true, `light` 0, it holds. Otherwise `light` grows by 1, and in the step it
would reach 60 the wall leaves the array. In the dark no wall is stepped; at the wake every
standing wall has `lit` false and `light` 0. A state whose `shadowWalls` is empty steps exactly as
before 0014.

`traces` is an array of `{ trace, light, found }`, one entry per trace of the map, in file order,
for the whole game (feature 0022, `game/trace`): a found trace stays in the array. `trace` is the
index of the trace among `tracesOf(map.rects)`; its box stays in the map, and `update` and the
scene rebuild the traces from `map.rects` on every call and keep them nowhere. `light` counts the
steps the light has been on it without a break, 0–59; `found` is the `tick` of the step in which
it was found, or `null` — never `undefined`. `createInitialState` reads the traces — and so
rejects a map with a trace the world data or the grid refuses (§6) — and starts every trace
`{ trace: n, light: 0, found: null }`. In an ordinary step, after the figure is stepped and next
to the walls, every trace not found is stepped against the stepped figure and the map **with**
the walls standing at the start of the step, as a creature is, so none is found through a wall.
A trace is in the light when the lamp is on, its box meets the cone (`meetsCone`), and no opaque
box lies on the line from (x, y − 4) to the pixel of the trace's box nearest to it. In the light
`light` grows by 1, and in the step it would reach 60 (`FIND_STEPS`) the trace is found: `found`
is the step's tick, `light` is 0. Out of the light `light` is 0. A found trace never changes
again. In the dark, with the journal open and with a dialogue open no trace is stepped; at the
wake every `light` is 0 and `found` is kept. The notice of a find is derived from `found` and
`tick` (§5) and is not in the state. A state whose `traces` is empty steps exactly as before 0022.

`solved` is an array of `{ region, at }`, the solved cases in the order they were solved
(feature 0024, `game/cases`): `region` names the case, `at` is the `tick` of the step that
solved it. It is empty at the start, and the wake keeps it. Whether a case is **ready** and
whether it is **solved** are rules, not fields: a case is ready when the map has at least one
trace of its region and every one of them is found (`isReady`), and solved when its region is
in `solved` (`isSolved`). Both read the **progress** of the cases, `progressOf(state, map)`: the
state's `traces`, the map's traces and `solved`, derived on every call and kept nowhere.

A region is **cleared** when it is in `solved` (feature 0025, `game/region`): `clearedAt(region,
solved)` is the `at` of its entry, or `null`. The state has no field for it. Whether a cell is
under shadow, which veil it shows and how far a clearing has come are derived from `solved`,
`tick` and the map's areas on every call (§5, §6).

`figures` is an array of `{ figure, facing }`, one entry per standing figure of the map, in file
order (feature 0017, `game/cast`). `figure` is the index of the figure among
`figuresOf(map.points)`; its place stays in the map and its pages in the cast, and neither is in
the state: `update` and the scene rebuild the figures from `map.points` on every call and keep
them nowhere. `facing` is `down` at the start and then where the figure turned the last time it
was spoken to. A standing figure never moves and is not solid. `dialogue` is `null`, or
`{ speaker, index, page }` (feature 0018): `speaker` is `'figure'` or `'mover'`, `index` the index
of the speaker in `figures` or in `movers`, and `page` the page shown, from 0.

A third kind of speaker is the story itself (feature 0026): `{ speaker: 'story', index, page }`,
with `index` the index of a **scene** in `SCENES` (`game/story`). A scene is pages with no figure
that speaks; `SCENES` holds one, the call, and `OPENING_SCENE`, 0, names it as the scene a game
opens with. A scene is no speaker of a step — nobody can walk up to it, and nobody turns. The
pages of a dialogue are `pagesOf(dialogue, speakers)` in `game/talk`: the scene's own for
`'story'`, else those of the speaker among the speakers of the step. A scene's dialogue is
stepped like any other. The game opens a scene, never a press; in this feature only `newGame`
does.

The **close** (feature 0034) is the second scene, `CLOSE_SCENE`, 1. `closeDue(tick, solved,
finished)` in `game/story` says whether it is due: `finished` is `null`, every case of `CASES`
is in `solved`, and `tick` minus the largest `at` among them is at least `CLOSE_DELAY_STEPS`,
300. In `update` a step in which it is due and the player is not being pushed opens it
(`openScene`) — after the dark, the help, the journal and an open dialogue have had the step,
and before a press of `journal` or of `talk` is looked at: `dialogue` becomes
`{ speaker: 'story', index: 1, page: 0 }`, the player's `frame` and `frameTimer` are 0, the
lamp is off, `tick` grows by 1 and the input is remembered; nothing else is stepped. `finished`
is the `tick` of the step that closes the scene, or `null`: `null` at the start and in a new
game, and the wake keeps it. Whether the close is due is derived in every step and is not in
the state.

The **first meeting** of feature 0026 — `FIRST_MEETING`, `figuresIn` and `isFirstMeeting` in
`game/cast` — is gone since feature 0039: every figure says its own pages from the first
talk, and `update` and the scene build the speakers from `figuresOf` and the hint alone. The
data protection officer's pages are three since feature 0036: `talk.officer-rules-3` names
Harmonic.

The **hints** (feature 0033) are data and one rule of `game/hints`. `HINT_FIGURE` names the
figure that says them, `intern`; `HINTS` holds per case — by its region — three pages, `start`,
`traces` and `source`; `HINT_DONE` is the page after the last case. `hintOf(progress)` picks
one (since feature 0039 it no longer asks for the lamp, and `HINT_LAMP` is gone): the first
case of `CASES` that is not solved decides — `start` while no trace of its
region is found, `traces` while some are and not all, `source` once it is ready (`isReady`);
with every case solved `HINT_DONE`. Since feature 0037 `HINTS` holds the three pages of Support
too, and the cases are asked in the order Sales, Marketing, Support.
`withHint(figures, hint)` gives `HINT_FIGURE` that one page
instead of its own; `update` and the scene both do it, so the box shows the
page the dialogue has. The hint is derived on every call and is not in the state; a talk with
the intern changes what a talk changes and nothing more. `CAST` holds the intern as its second
member, with `talk.intern-sales-start` as the page he has before a step has picked one — the
first hint of a new game (feature 0039).

`held` is the last step's input, a plain `GameInput`, every action false at the start
(`NO_INPUT`); it replaced `talkHeld`, the `talk` of the last step, with feature 0020, because the
journal needs presses of three actions. An action is **pressed** in a step when the input holds
it and `held` does not (`game/player`, `pressed`; `talkPressed` in `game/talk` reads it for
`talk`). `journal` is `null`, or `{ tab }` with one of `JOURNAL_TABS` — `creatures`, `cases`,
`policy` — (feature 0020, `game/journal`). What a tab shows is not in the state: the **entries**
of the creatures tab are the species of which at least one was captured, in `SPECIES` order,
derived from `captured` in every frame by `entriesOf`. The entries of the cases tab are the
regions of which at least one trace is found, in the order of the world data, each with the
number of its found traces and of all its traces on the map — derived from `traces` and the map's
traces in every frame by `caseEntries` (feature 0022). A solved case's entry also has its
outcome, `outcomeOf`; the policy's blocks are the solved cases' blocks in the order they were
solved, `policyBlocks` — both derived from `solved` in every frame (feature 0024).
`help` is a boolean, `false` at the start: whether the help is open (feature 0021,
`game/help`). The rows the help shows and the label that names its key (feature 0031) are not
in the state: they are constants of `render/draw-help`, and no rule of the game reads them.

The **speakers** of a step (feature 0018, `speakersOf` in `game/talk`) are the standing figures in
file order, then the movers of the kind `person` whose route is in `COLLEAGUES` (`game/cast`), in
the order of `movers`; each is where it is in this step — a figure at its point, a colleague at its
`x` and `y` in the state, not rounded. Like the figures they are rebuilt on every call and kept
nowhere. A person whose route has no lines, a dog, a vacuum and a drone are no speakers.
What a colleague says follows from the progress of the cases (feature 0024, `pagesIn`): the
source of a solved case says its pages "afterwards", the source of a case that is ready and not
solved says its answer, and everyone else says the everyday pages. `update` and the scene pass
the same `progressOf(state, map)` to `speakersOf`, so the box never shows a page the dialogue
does not have.

A step is one of five kinds, in this order. **Dark:** with `dark` above 0 it is `stepDark`, as
above, and sets `held`; `journal` and `help` do nothing. **Help:** with the help open, `tick`
grows by 1 and `held` is set; a press of `help` sets `help` to `false`; nothing else changes —
not the player, the lamp, the creatures, the movers, the walls, the traces or a figure, not the
page of an open dialogue and not the tab of the open journal (`stepHelp`). Otherwise a press of
`help` opens it before anything else is looked at, over an open journal and an open dialogue
too, whatever else the input holds: `help` becomes `true`, `tick` grows by 1, `held` is set, and
nothing else changes in this step (`openHelp`). **Journal:** with the journal open, `tick` grows
by 1 and `held` is set; a press of `journal` sets `journal` to `null`; otherwise a press of
`right` while `left` is not pressed turns to the next tab, a press of `left` while `right` is not
pressed to the one before, and at either end the tab stays. Nothing else changes — not the
player, the lamp, the creatures, the movers, the walls, the traces or a figure. **Dialogue:**
with a dialogue open, `tick` grows by 1, `held` is set, and a press turns to the next page or,
on the last page, sets `dialogue` to `null`; nothing else changes — not the player, the lamp, the
creatures, the movers, the walls or the traces — and a press of `journal` does nothing. The
step whose press closes the source's answer of a ready case that is not solved **solves** it
(`answeredCase`, `solve`, feature 0024): besides the above it adds `{ region, at }` with the
step's `tick` to `solved`, and sets `overview` to `{ current: max + 1, max: max + 1 }`. The same
step earns the upgrade of the region, if it has one (`earn`, feature 0029). The same
step takes what is left of the region's shadow with it (`clearRegion`, feature 0025): every
entry of `shadowWalls` whose wall belongs to the region leaves the list, as a lifted wall does,
and every creature whose `home` lies in an area of the region (`inArea`: inside the rectangle,
the left and the upper edge included) leaves `creatures`, wherever it is; `captured` does not
change, and a creature at home outside the region stays. The step changes
`tick`, `held`, `dialogue`, `solved`, `overview`, `shadowWalls` and `creatures` and nothing
else; no other step solves a case, and a solved case is not solved again. The wake brings back
only the creatures still in `creatures`, so none that left this way. Closing any other
dialogue of a figure or a colleague changes nothing more: since feature 0039 no talk hands
anything over. **Ordinary:** otherwise, a press of `journal` opens the journal on `creatures`, whatever else
the input holds: `tick` grows by 1, `held` is set, and nothing else changes in this step — a
figure in mid-step, a push and a lamp that is on stay as they are and go on when it closes
(`openJournal`). This comes before a press of `talk` is looked at. Otherwise a press of `talk` while the input does not
hold `lamp`, the player is not pushed and a speaker stands at most 40 px (`TALK_DISTANCE`) from
the player's feet and in sight opens a dialogue with the nearest one — the first in the speakers'
order of two equally near — on page 0, and nothing else is stepped. In sight (`inSight`) is the
lamp's `sightBlocked` between the centres of the two boxes, (x, y − 4) of each, against the opaque
boxes of the map as loaded. The two turn to each other (left or right when the horizontal distance
is the larger one, up or down otherwise, unchanged on the same spot), the player stands at phase
0, timer 0, the lamp is off, and `tick` grows by 1. A colleague that is spoken to gets `frame` 0,
`frameTimer` 0 and the facing toward the player, and nothing else of it changes, so `game/mover`
steps it on unchanged after the dialogue. Without such a press the
step is the one described above, with one addition (feature 0027, `takeRest`): when `talk` is
pressed, `lamp` is not held — as `update` counts it —, the player is not pushed and a rest is in
reach (§6), the player takes it before anything else of the step. The result is `shadow` when
the rest's cell is under shadow — `veilOf` on the areas not cleared, above 0 —, else `full` when
`overview.current` equals `overview.max`, else `restored`, and only then `overview.current`
becomes `overview.max`. `rested` is set to `{ at: tick + 1, result }`, and the step goes on from
there as an ordinary one: the hit of this step, if there is one, takes its point from the
filled overview. A speaker in reach comes first, because the opening of a dialogue is looked at
before; so do the journal and the help. Every step, of every kind, sets `held` from its input, so a
key held down is pressed once. The wake keeps `figures` and `held` and has no dialogue and no
journal, and its help is closed: the help does not open in the dark, and no dark begins while it
is open. A state without speakers, in which `talk` is never held, steps exactly as before 0018;
a state in which `journal` is never held steps exactly as before 0020; a state in which `help`
is never held steps exactly as before 0021.

Static content — the tile map, tileset properties, sprite definitions, the language table — is
not state. It is loaded once and passed in read-only. The rule of thumb: if it would be saved in a
save state, it is in `GameState`; if it ships with the build, it is content. The player's
settings are neither: the language (feature 0019) is held by `main.ts`, not saved with a game and
not shipped, and reaches only the scene (§5, §8).

`update(state, input, content)` advances the state by exactly one logic step and returns the new
state. `input` is `GameInput`: the four movement actions, `lamp`, `talk`, `journal` and `help`,
as booleans (§4);
`content` is the loaded map. Whether it mutates in place or returns a copy is an implementation
choice per module; what it may not do is read anything but its arguments or write anything but
its result.

## 3. The loop

Fixed step at 60 Hz (INV-3), rendering once per browser frame:

```
elapsed = snap(elapsed)             within 0.25 ms of ½, 1 or 2 steps: exactly that
accumulator += min(elapsed, cap)    counted in steps, not milliseconds
input = readInput()                 once per frame, before the steps
while accumulator >= step:          step = 1/60 s
    state = update(state, input, content)
    accumulator -= step
render(state, content)
```

The cap limits catch-up after a stall to five steps; anything beyond is dropped, so a background
tab does not fast-forward the game on return. Rendering draws the latest state without
interpolation — with integer pixel positions there is nothing to interpolate. The loop is a pure
function of a clock and is tested with a fake one (`engine/loop`: `advance` is the pure core,
`runLoop` drives it from a frame scheduler).

**Snapping.** On a 60 Hz or 120 Hz display a frame lasts almost exactly one step or half of one.
Without snapping the remainder sits on the threshold, and clock jitter of a tenth of a
millisecond gives none and then two steps in a row — the moving picture stops and jumps. Snapped,
a 60 Hz display advances exactly one step per frame and a 120 Hz display one every second frame;
on 59.94 Hz the game runs 0.1 % slow, which nobody sees. Other rates, such as 144 Hz, are not
snapped and show the uneven cadence that a fixed step without interpolation implies.

**The card counts steps.** The card of a new game (feature 0036) lasts 180 steps, not three
seconds of a clock: steps the cap drops after a stall are not counted, so a tab that was in the
background shows the card for its whole length when it comes back.

**Steps, not milliseconds.** The accumulator counts steps. In milliseconds floating point loses a
step: five capped steps of `1000 / 60` ms, subtracted one by one, leave 16.66666666666666 and run
only four.

**Input once per frame.** `runLoop` takes three callbacks: `readInput`, `update` and `render`.
It calls `readInput` once per frame, before that frame's steps, and hands the result to every
step, so every step of a frame sees the same actions; a frame without a step still reads it. The
loop is the one place that knows where a frame begins. `main.ts` supplies `readInput` (§4) and
closes `update` over the map.

## 4. Input

`engine/input` keeps the current keyboard state: which keys are down, by `KeyboardEvent.code`, so
the physical key counts and the layout does not — W is W on QWERTZ and on QWERTY. On `blur` of
the window and when the document turns hidden (`visibilitychange`), every key is released,
because no `keyup` will come for what is held. A `keydown` with `repeat` changes nothing. That
matters after a release: a key still held when the page regains focus keeps repeating, and must
not press itself again, so the figure stands until the key is really pressed again. The keyboard
does not keep which keys went down this step, the edge a single press needs. Talking (feature
0017) is the first that needs a press, and the game derives it in the state: `held` remembers
the whole input of the last step (§2; `talkHeld`, the last step's `talk`, until feature 0020).
The keyboard still keeps no edge, and it reads the held keys once per frame, so a key that goes
down and up within one frame is not seen.

The game reads named actions — `up`, `down`, `left`, `right`, `lamp`, `talk`, `journal` and
`help` — never key codes. The mapping from keys to actions lives in one table in `main.ts`:
`up` ← ArrowUp or W, `down` ← ArrowDown or S, `left` ← ArrowLeft or A, `right` ← ArrowRight or
D, `lamp` ← Space, `talk` ← E or Enter, `journal` ← J (feature 0020), `help` ← H (feature
0021), `fullscreen` ← F, `language` ← L.
Once per frame, before the steps (§3), `main.ts` resolves the held keys through the table with
`resolveActions`, a pure function in `engine/input`: an action is on when any of its keys is
down. The game gets the eight actions as booleans and nothing else. A gamepad (deferred,
`docs/plan.md` §5) and remappable keys are additions to the table, not changes to the game.

A bound key pressed without Ctrl, Alt or Cmd belongs to the game: `main.ts` prevents its default,
so the arrow keys never scroll the page and Space neither scrolls nor clicks a focused control.
With Ctrl, Alt or Cmd the browser keeps its own shortcut.
`fullscreen` is toggled in `main.ts` on the `keydown` of F without `repeat` and without Ctrl,
Alt or Cmd; it never reaches the game. `language` (feature 0019) is handled the same way on the
`keydown` of L: `main.ts` sets the language to `nextLanguage` of it, notes the state's `tick` of
the last frame as `switchedAt`, and sets the `lang` attribute of the page's root element. A held
key switches once; with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd the browser keeps its shortcut and the language stays;
before the content has loaded the key does nothing. The game still gets its eight actions.

The input is what the keys say, not what the player can do with them: `lamp` is on while Space
is held, also while the card is shown or a dialogue is open. `update` is the place that knows
whose step it is; `held` keeps the input as it came.

## 5. The renderer interface

`render/renderer.ts` declares what the game needs drawn, in game pixels on a 320x180 surface:
tiles from a tileset, sprites from a sprite sheet, rectangles in a palette colour (from feature
0009 on, the lids of the dark). Text needs nothing of its own: a glyph is a sprite of the font
sheet (feature 0016). `render/canvas2d/` implements it with the Canvas 2D API, draws to an offscreen 320x180
canvas, and blits that onto the visible canvas at the largest integer factor that fits, counted
in device pixels (`visual.md` §2). `render/scale` computes that layout as a pure function of the
page area and the device pixel ratio; `main.ts` reads both every frame, so resize, zoom,
fullscreen and a move to another monitor share one path, and the renderer resizes the canvas
only when the layout changes. All positions the renderer receives are integers; the camera
offset is rounded before drawing, never after. `drawLayer` draws cell (c, r) at
(16c − camera.x, 16r − camera.y), and only the columns ⌊camera.x / 16⌋ … ⌊(camera.x + 319) / 16⌋
and the rows ⌊camera.y / 16⌋ … ⌊(camera.y + 179) / 16⌋, cut to the map — at most 21 × 13 cells
per layer; a cell at the view's edge is clipped by the surface. Away from the map's edges the
figure sits at screen (152, 70) for every fractional position, because the camera and the
sprite round the same value.

`drawSprite(sheet, frame, x, y)` draws frame `frame` of a sprite sheet — frames of the sheet's own
`frameWidth × frameHeight` in one row; the figure's are 16x24, the lamp's 48x48, the wider cone's 72x72 — with its
top-left corner at (x, y), from the decoded image like `drawTile`, smoothing off.
`render/draw-scene` holds the draw order of a frame: clear, then `ground`, `detail`, the veil
over the cells under shadow (feature 0023) and over those of a region that is clearing (feature
0025), the lamp's cone when the lamp is on, the standing shadow walls in array order (feature 0014), the traces
the light is on in array order (feature 0022), the ground movers whose feet y is at most the player's in array order, the standing figures whose feet y is
at most the player's (feature 0017), the creatures in array order, the player, the other
standing figures, the other ground movers in array order, `above` (§6), the drones (feature
0015), the hearts of overview, the help label (feature 0031), the dialogue box while a dialogue is open or else the notice
(features 0016, 0017), the help while it is open (feature 0021), the lids of the dark. A ground mover is a person, a dog or a vacuum; comparing the feet makes walking past a colleague read as depth, and a
drone flies over the tops of `above`. The player's sprite has its top-left corner at
(round(x) − 8 − camera.x, round(y) − 24 − camera.y): the feet are the bottom centre of the
sprite, so they sit on the tile grid and the figure stands on its tile. Its frame in the sheet is
facing × 5 + phase (§7). The cone's frame is drawn with its top-left corner at the apex,
(round(x), round(y) − 12), minus the frame's apex pixel (`game/lamp`, `FRAME_APEX`: down (24, 0),
up (24, 47), left (47, 24), right (0, 24)), minus the camera; its frame is the facing's index.
Cone and figure round the same values, so they agree pixel for pixel, and the cone lies under the
figure. While `upgrades` holds `wide-cone` (feature 0029) the cone is drawn from
`lampWideSheet` instead, at the same place in the order, its 72x72 frame placed by
`WIDE_FRAME_APEX` — down (36, 0), up (36, 71), left (71, 36), right (0, 36) — in the same way
(`lampFrameOrigin(player, camera, apexes)`). A creature's frame has its top-left corner at (round(x) − 8 − camera.x, round(y) − 16 −
camera.y); its frame is 2 while `light` is above 0, else ⌊tick / 15⌋ mod 2 (feature 0008). A
shy creature that is looked at and not lit shows frame 0 and holds still (feature 0032,
`creatureFrame(creature, tick, watched)`); the scene asks `isWatched` against the map with the
walls standing, as `update` does, and builds that map only when a shy creature is in the state.

The shadow walls (feature 0014) lie on the floor like the cone, under every figure. A wall whose
box is (x, y, w, h) is drawn in 16 px segments from the corner of its box: a horizontal wall
(wider than high) as w / 16 frames of orientation 0 at (x + 16k − camera.x, y − camera.y), a
vertical one as h / 16 frames of orientation 1 at (x − camera.x, y + 16k − camera.y), k from 0.
The frame is orientation × 8 + look (§7). The look: while `light` is 1–19 look 0, 20–39 look 6,
40–59 look 7, still, whatever the density; `lit` with `light` 0, a wall that holds, look
(density − 1) × 2, still; otherwise look (density − 1) × 2 + ⌊tick / 15⌋ mod 2, a flicker four
times a second. A lifted wall is not drawn. The walls are rebuilt from `map.rects` every frame,
as in `update`.

The shadow over the regions (feature 0023). `SceneContent` holds two more images: the shadow
tileset `shadowTileset` — the tileset's sheet in greys, same size, same ids (§7) — and the veil
sheet `veilSheet`, three 16x16 frames. Every frame the scene reads the areas from `map.rects`
with `areasOf` (`game/region`, §6) and asks `veilOf` for each cell it draws. On `ground`,
`detail` and `above`, `drawLayer` draws a cell under shadow from the shadow tileset — the same id
at the same place — and every other cell from the tileset. After `detail` and before the cone,
`drawVeil` draws on every cell under shadow the view touches the veil's frame density − 1, with
its top-left corner at (16 × column − camera.x, 16 × row − camera.y), row by row, empty cells
included. Everything after it — the cone, the walls, the traces, the movers, the figures, the
creatures, the player, `above` — is drawn as before, so the people keep their colours in a grey
room and `above` gets no veil. A map without areas passes no shadow to `drawLayer` and draws no
veil: it is drawn exactly as before.

The cleared region (feature 0025). The veil sheet has five frames, and the scene asks two
questions per cell. Which tileset: `drawLayer` gets `veilOf` on `shadowedAreas(areas, solved)`,
the areas of the regions that are not cleared, so a cell of a cleared region is drawn from the
tileset from the frame after the solving step. Which veil: `drawVeil` gets
`shownVeil(areas, solved, tick, column, row)` and draws, as before, the frame veil − 1. Over a
cell under shadow that is the veil of its density, 1 to 3. Over a cell of a region cleared e =
`tick` − `at` steps ago it is `clearingVeil(density, e)`: the density for e below 40, 4 for e
below 80, 5 for e below 120 (`CLEAR_STEPS`), and 0 — no veil — from then on; the veils 4 and 5
are the frames 3 and 4 of the sheet. Where a cleared area and one under shadow overlap, the
shadow counts; of two clearing areas, the first in the file. A region without shadow has no
veil at any time.

The traces (feature 0022) lie on their devices like the walls on the floor, under every figure.
A trace with `light` L above 0 is drawn with frame ⌊(L − 1) / 20⌋ of the trace sheet — 0, 1 or
2, small, larger, largest (`traceFrame`, `TRACE_LOOKS` 3) — with its top-left corner at
(x − camera.x, y − camera.y) of its box. A trace with `light` 0 is not drawn, and a found one
never. The traces are rebuilt from `map.rects` every frame, as in `update`.

The movers (feature 0015): a person's frame has its top-left corner at (round(x) − 8 − camera.x,
round(y) − 24 − camera.y) and is facing × 5 + `frame`, as the figure's, in its own sheet
`person-(look + 1)`. A dog's, a vacuum's and a drone's frame has its top-left corner at
(round(x) − 8 − camera.x, round(y) − 16 − camera.y). A dog's frame is `look` × 12 + facing × 3 +
`frame`; a vacuum's is ⌊tick / 30⌋ mod 2, its light on and off; a drone's is ⌊tick / 4⌋ mod 2,
its rotors turning, and the drone is drawn 1 px higher while ⌊tick / 30⌋ mod 2 is 1. The blink of
a safe figure does not touch the movers.

The hearts of overview (features 0009, 0012) are drawn on the surface, not shifted by the
camera: heart i, for i below `max`, has its top-left corner at (4 + 10i, 4), with frame 0 when i is below
`current`, else frame 1. With `dark` = d above 0 two rectangles in `#181425` are drawn last,
(0, 0, 320, L) and (0, 180 − L, 320, L), with L = min(3 × min(d, 90 − d), 90): they close in 30
steps, stay closed for 30 and open in 30. While `grace` is above 0 the player's sprite is left
out in every frame in which ⌊grace / 4⌋ is odd; the cone is drawn as before.

The help label (feature 0031, `drawHelpLabel` in `render/draw-help`) is drawn on the surface,
not shifted by the camera, directly after the hearts, in every frame in which the scene draws
the world: nothing of the state decides it — not `tick`, not a dialogue, not a notice, not the
help, not the dark. With n the number of characters of the key `help.label` in the language of
the settings, `helpLabelPlace(n)` gives the frame, a rectangle (311 − 6n, 4, 6n + 5, 14) in
`#3a4466`; the field, (312 − 6n, 5, 6n + 3, 12) in `#181425`; and the origin of the words,
(314 − 6n, 6). In German that is (269, 4, 47, 14), (270, 5, 45, 12) and (272, 6); in English
(275, 4, 41, 14), (276, 5, 39, 12) and (278, 6). The frame ends 4 px from the surface's right
edge in every language. A label of 8 characters, the most the rule allows, begins at x = 263,
right of seven hearts, which end at x = 71; the label ends at y = 17, above the help's box. The
journal's screen has no label: its third tab has that corner.

The notice (feature 0016) is drawn on the surface, not shifted by the camera, after the hearts
and the help label and before the lids: when `game/notice` names one, a rectangle (8, 158, 304, 18) in `#181425`,
then its line, a string of n characters from (160 − 3n, 162). The bar leaves 4 px around the
10 px line and holds at most 49 characters; a test holds every notice of the table to that in
both languages. Text is drawn by `render/draw-text`, the one function that draws it: it takes a
`TextKey` and a language, never a string, and draws character n of line k of the key's string as
a sprite of the font sheet with its top-left corner at (x + 6n, y + 12k), its frame the
character's place in the glyph order (§7). A line break starts the next line, 12 px lower — a
line is 10 px high and 2 px lie between two lines (feature 0017). A space is not drawn and only
takes its 6 px; a character the font does not have is drawn as `?`. There is no wrapping.
`game/notice` knows two notices (feature 0022): the wall that holds comes first; else, while a
trace has `found` not `null` and `tick` − `found` < `TRACE_NOTICE_STEPS` (120, two seconds),
`notice.trace-found`. A second find starts the two seconds again. A third notice joins them
(feature 0024): while a solved case has `tick` − `at` < `CASE_NOTICE_STEPS` (120, two seconds),
`notice.case-solved`. The order is the wall that holds, then a solved case, then a found trace.
`render/draw-text` also has
`drawNumber(renderer, font, value, x, y)`: the decimal digits of a whole number of 0 or more,
digit n with its top-left corner at (x + 6n, y). It takes a number and nothing else; a number is
no text of a language and needs no key.

`drawScene(renderer, state, content, settings)` takes the player's settings as its fourth
argument (feature 0019): `{ language, switchedAt }`, which `main.ts` holds and hands over with
every frame. `SceneContent` holds loaded content only. Every text of the scene is drawn in
`language`. The language line, the key `notice.language`, is drawn like the notice — the bar
(8, 158, 304, 18) in `#181425` and the line centred in it — when `switchedAt` is not null and
0 ≤ `tick` − `switchedAt` < `LANGUAGE_NOTICE_STEPS` (120, two seconds), no dialogue is open and
no notice shows. The dialogue box comes first, then the notice, then the language line: where
a text is on screen, that text changes with the language and is the answer to a switch.
`quietLine` names the line of the bar where no dialogue is open and no notice shows: none while
the help is open, else the language line when it is due, else none. The start hint of feature
0021, a line that named the help's key for ten seconds, had the last place in the bar until
feature 0031 replaced it with the help label. The lids of the dark close over the line as over
everything.

A standing figure (feature 0017) is drawn from its own sheet, `<id>.png`, like a colleague who
stands: frame facing × 5, top-left corner at (round(x) − 8 − camera.x, round(y) − 24 −
camera.y). The figures are rebuilt from `map.points` every frame, as in `update`. While a dialogue
is open the dialogue box is drawn in the notice's place, on the surface and not shifted by the
camera, and no notice is drawn. The page is the speaker's (feature 0018) or the scene's (feature
0026): the scene finds it with `pagesOf` among the speakers of the frame — built with
`progressOf(state, map)` (feature 0024) and the hint of the moment (feature 0033), as
in `update` — and draws no box when neither the map nor the story has it.
The box is a rectangle (8, 134, 304, 42) in `#181425` — the notice's bar
grown upwards to three lines, with the same bottom edge —, the page's lines left-aligned from
(12, 138), and, while ⌊tick / 30⌋ is even, the marker: three rectangles in `#ffffff`, (299, 169,
5, 1), (300, 170, 3, 1) and (301, 171, 1, 1), a small triangle that points down and blinks once a
second, on every page. A line of 47 characters ends at x = 294, left of the marker.

The card (feature 0036, `render/draw-card`). While `presented` is `null`, `drawScene` calls
`drawCard` and returns, before it looks at the journal or the help: `clear` to the page
background, then `card.presents` with its top at y = 74 and `card.title` at y = 94, each from
x = `centredX(n)`, 160 − 3n for the n characters of the line in the language of the settings —
112 and 133 in German, 121 and 133 in English. Nothing else is drawn: no layer, no figure, no
heart, no label of the help, no box, no bar and no lid.

The journal (feature 0020, `render/draw-journal`). While `journal` is not `null`, `drawScene`

## 6. Maps and the Tiled pipeline

Maps are authored in Tiled and saved as JSON (`.tmj`) directly under `public/maps/`; the file
Tiled writes is the file the game loads. Tilesets are external (`.tsj`) under `public/tiles/`
beside their PNG. Nothing is converted at build time. The floor, `public/maps/floor.tmj`,
108x80 tiles, is generated from `tools/floor-layout.ts` until the art pass (§7) and opens in
Tiled like any other map.

Fixed layer names (INV-7): `ground` and `detail` are drawn below the player, `above` is drawn
over the player, `objects` is an object layer that holds the spawn point, named points —
creatures and route points today, later doors, NPCs and triggers — and named rectangles, the
shadow walls (feature 0014), the traces (feature 0022) and the areas of the regions (feature
0023) today. Whether a tile is solid, and which part of it,
is a property of the tile in the tileset (feature 0010): the boolean tile property `solid` makes
the whole tile solid, the box (0, 0, 16, 16); a tile without it may carry rectangles drawn in
Tiled's tile collision editor, its `objectgroup` in the `.tsj`, each a box in tile-local pixels.
A wall thinner than a tile is such a rectangle. The loader builds the solid boxes from both.
The boolean tile property `low` (feature 0011) marks a tile whose boxes stop feet but not light,
like a desk or a partition; a tile that is `low` and has no box is rejected.
**Loading.** `main.ts` loads the map, its tileset and the tileset's image before the first frame;
until then the page shows its background. Every path inside a file is resolved against that file
— the map names `../tiles/placeholder.tsj`, the tileset names `placeholder.png` — and all three
come from the game's own origin (INV-5). `engine/assets` does the fetching; `resolvePath` uses a
`file:///` base, because the build may contain no `http://` URL. The map is content, not state
(§2).

**Parsing.** `engine/tiled` holds three pure functions over parsed JSON. `tilesetSource` finds
the map's one external tileset; `parseTileset` reads its tile size, columns, tile count, image
and per tile its boxes — the full tile for `solid`, else its collision rectangles, else none —
as `Tileset.boxes`, and per tile whether it is `low` as `Tileset.low`; `parseTileMap` reads the map against the parsed tileset and
returns a `TileMap`. It reads only what the game uses: of the map `orientation`, `infinite`,
`width`, `height`, `tilewidth`, `tileheight`, `tilesets` and `layers`; of a layer `name`, `type`,
`data`, `encoding`, `compression` and, for `objects`, the objects' `name`, `point`, `x`, `y`,
`width`, `height`, `rotation`, the fields that make an object some other shape, and the one
custom property `pause` (feature 0015). `TileMap.points` are the point objects other
than the spawn, as `{ name, x, y }` in file order, with `pause` added when the object carries
the property. `TileMap.rects` are the rectangle objects — an object with none of `point`,
`ellipse`, `polygon`, `polyline`, `text` and `gid` — as `{ name, x, y, width, height }` in file
order, empty on a map without them (feature 0014). `engine/` does not know what a name means,
`game/` does. The game knows the
species' names, `wake` (feature 0009), the route points of the movers (feature 0015) and the
points of the standing figures (feature 0017). Everything else Tiled writes is
ignored — a layer hidden in Tiled is drawn by the game. It does
not try to be a general Tiled importer.

**The TileMap.** Width and height in tiles; `ground`, `detail` and `above` as row-major arrays
of tileset ids, where gid 0 becomes `EMPTY` (−1) and gid `g` becomes `g − firstgid`; an absent
`detail` or `above` is all `EMPTY`. The `spawn` point is in pixels, and so are the named
`points`. `solids` is row-major, width × height: per cell the boxes of its tiles on the three
layers, moved to map pixels, so collision has one list per cell to ask; a cell with no box is
open. A box is half-open: (x, y, width, height) covers [x, x + width) × [y, y + height).
`opaque` is built the same way from the tiles that are not low; a cell with no such box shares
the empty array. `moveBox` reads `solids`, for the figure and the creatures alike; `isLit` hands
`sightBlocked` a grid whose boxes are `opaque`, so the light stops at walls, closed doors and
cabinets and passes over everything lower than a person (feature 0011). `isWatched` asks the
same grid whether the figure sees a creature (feature 0032).

**What the loader accepts.** An orthogonal, finite map with 16x16 tiles, at least 20x12 tiles
(320x180 px, the surface); exactly one external `.tsj` tileset with 16x16 tiles; layer data in
Tiled's default CSV, an array of whole numbers of length `width × height`; the layers `ground`
and `objects`, and optionally `detail` and `above`, each at most once; a spawn inside the map and
outside every solid box; every named point inside the map and outside every solid box. A point
in the open part of a wall cell is accepted.

**What it rejects.** Anything else stops the game before it starts, with an error that names the
file and the problem. First the tileset: not exactly one tileset in the map, an embedded tileset,
a source that does not end in `.tsj`, a `.tsj` that does not load, a tileset whose tiles are not
16x16, a tileset tile with an `animation`, and per tile, in this order: a collision object that
is not a rectangle (an ellipse, a point, a polygon, a polyline, a text or a tile object), a
rotated one, a rectangle that is not whole pixels inside the tile — a fractional number, a width
or height below 1, any part outside [0, 16) × [0, 16) — and a tile that is `solid` and has
collision rectangles, and a tile that is `low` and has no collision box (`tile N is low but
has no collision box`). Then the map, in this order:

- an orientation other than orthogonal, or an infinite map;
- a tile size other than 16x16;
- a map under 20x12 tiles;
- per layer, in file order: a name outside the four, a type that does not match the name
  (`objects` an object layer, the others tile layers), a name used twice, an `encoding` other than
  CSV or any `compression`, a cell count other than `width × height`, a cell that is not a whole
  number, a gid with one of Tiled's four flip or rotation bits set, a gid outside the tileset;
- a missing `ground` or `objects` layer;
- no point object named `spawn` in `objects`, or more than one;
- a spawn outside the map, or inside a solid box of its cell;
- a point object without a name;
- a named point outside the map, or inside a solid box of its cell;
- a point whose `pause` property is not of type `int` (`point "dog:hr:1" has a pause that is not
  an int property`);
- after the rules for points, an object that is neither a point nor a rectangle (`layer
  "objects" has an object that is neither a point nor a rectangle`), a rectangle without a name
  (`layer "objects" has a rectangle object without a name`), a rotated rectangle (`rectangle
  "shadow:sales" is rotated`), and a rectangle with a fractional number, a width or height below
  1 or any part outside the map (`rectangle "shadow:sales" (x, y, w, h) is not whole pixels
  inside the map`) (feature 0014).

Last, the tileset's image must load.

**Route points** (feature 0015). A point whose name has a `:` is a route point of a mover:
`<kind>:<room>:<n>`, with `<kind>` one of `person`, `dog`, `vacuum`, `drone`, `<room>` one of
`hr`, `accounting`, `development`, `reception`, `break-room`, `sales`, `marketing`, `support`, and
`n` from 1 without a gap. A route is the two to four points of one `<kind>:<room>`; its `pause`,
0 to 600 steps, is the custom `int` property `pause`, 0 when absent. `game/mover` builds the
routes in the order of each route's first point in the file and, in `createInitialState`,
refuses the map — each with its message — for a name with a kind but no room or number (`point
dog:hr is not a route point`), an unknown room (`point dog:cellar:1 names no room`), a pause
outside 0–600 or not whole (`point dog:hr:1 has pause 700, 0 to 600 are allowed`), a number used
twice (`route dog:hr has two points 2`), fewer than two or more than four points (`route dog:hr
has 1 point, 2 to 4 are allowed`), a gap (`route dog:hr has no point 2`), a leg that is neither
horizontal nor vertical, the closing leg from the last point to the first included (`route
dog:hr leg 2 is not straight`), and, for every kind but `drone`, a leg on which the figure's box
`[x − 6, x + 6) × [y − 8, y)` meets a solid box (`route dog:hr leg 2 crosses a solid tile (c,
r)`, the first such cell row by row). A drone's legs may cross anything; its points, like every
named point, lie outside every solid box. `creaturesFrom` skips route points.

**Figure points** (feature 0017). A point named `figure:<id>` is where a figure of the cast
stands; its position is the feet. It is no route point and makes no creature: `isRoutePoint` and
`creaturesFrom` skip it. `figuresOf` (`game/cast`) reads the figures in file order and, in
`createInitialState`, refuses the map — each with its message — for an id the cast does not have
(`figure point figure:cellar names no figure`) and for two points with the same id (`more than one
point object "figure:officer"`). A map without such points has no figures; a figure of the cast
without a point is simply not there. The floor has two: `figure:officer`, and since feature
0033 `figure:intern`.

**Kinds of rectangle** (features 0022, 0023, 0027). A rectangle has a kind by the prefix of its
name: `shadow:` a shadow wall, `trace:` a trace, `region:` an area of a region, `rest:` a rest.
`game/rects` is the one place that knows the prefixes, and holds `isCell`, the test for a box
that is exactly one cell;
`rectsOf(rects, prefix)` gives the rectangles of one kind in file order and refuses a map with a
rectangle of no known kind (`unknown rectangle object "door"`), whichever kind is asked for.
`game/shadow`, `game/trace`, `game/region` and `game/rest` read their rectangles through it and
skip the other kinds; none imports another's rectangles.

**Shadow walls** (feature 0014). A rectangle named `shadow:<region>` is a shadow wall: a strip
4 px thick on the top or the left edge of whole cells, where a doorway's wall strip is missing. A
region may have more than one. `game/shadow` reads the walls from `map.rects` in file order as
`{ region, density, box }`, the density from `game/world`, skipping the traces, and, in
`createInitialState`, refuses the map — each with its message — for a rectangle of no known kind
(`unknown rectangle object "door"`), a region not in the world data (`shadow wall shadow:cellar names no region`), a region
of density 0 (`shadow wall shadow:reception stands in a region without shadow`), and a box whose
`x` or `y` is no multiple of 16 or whose size is neither 4 px high and a multiple of 16 px wide
nor 4 px wide and a multiple of 16 px high (`shadow wall shadow:sales (x, y, w, h) is no strip on
a cell edge`). No coordinate of a wall is in `src/` (INV-7).

**Traces** (feature 0022). A rectangle named `trace:<region>` is a trace: exactly one cell, 16x16
with its corner on the grid, on the cell of the device it lies on. A region may have more than
one. `game/trace` reads the traces from `map.rects` in file order as `{ region, box }`, skipping
the walls, and, in `createInitialState`, refuses the map — each with its message — for a region
not in the world data (`trace trace:cellar names no region`) and a box that is not one cell
(`trace trace:sales (1330, 448, 16, 16) is not one cell`). A map without such rectangles has no
traces. No coordinate of a trace is in `src/` (INV-7); `tools/floor-layout.ts` names the cells
of the floor's fifteen, `TRACES`: five of Sales (feature 0022), five of Marketing (feature 0028)
and five of Support (feature 0037).

**Areas of the regions** (feature 0023). A rectangle named `region:<id>` is an area of a region
of the world data: whole cells, its corner on the grid, its width and its height multiples of 16
and above 0. A region may have several areas; they share the name. `game/region` reads them with
`areasOf(map.rects)` in file order as `{ region, density, box }`, the density from `game/world`,
and refuses the map — each with its message — for a region not in the world data (`region
region:cellar names no region`) and a box that is not whole cells (`region region:sales (1264,
418, 448, 416) is not whole cells`). `createInitialState` calls it once, so a faulty area stops
the game before it starts; the state keeps nothing of the areas. A map without such rectangles
has no areas, and a region without an area on a map is simply not on it. `veilOf(areas, column,
row)` is the veil of a cell: the density of the area it lies in, the largest where areas
overlap, 0 for a cell in no area or only in areas of density 0. A cell with a veil above 0 is
under shadow — asked of the areas that are not cleared, `shadowedAreas(areas, solved)` (feature
0025, §5): the areas of a region whose case is solved drop out of it. No coordinate of an area is in `src/` (INV-7); `tools/floor-layout.ts` builds the
floor's ten, `REGION_AREAS`, from its rooms, each with its upper and its left wall (`areaBox`).

**Rests** (feature 0027). A rectangle named `rest:<what>` is a rest: exactly one cell, on the
cell of the thing it is. `game/rest` reads them with `restsOf(map.rects)` in file order as
`{ what, box }` and refuses the map for a box that is not one cell (`rest rest:coffee (568, 418,
16, 16) is not one cell`); `createInitialState` calls it once, and the state keeps nothing of the
rests. A rest is **in reach** (`restInReach`) when the centre of its cell is at most
`REST_DISTANCE`, 32 px, from the player's feet and no opaque box of the map as loaded lies on
the line from the centre of the player's box, (x, y − 4), to that centre — the lamp's
`sightBlocked`. So a machine in the wall's row of its room is reached from inside the room and
not from the corridor behind the wall. Of two in reach the nearer counts, of two equally near
the first in the file. `restUnderShadow` asks the rest's cell of the areas not cleared (§5).
No coordinate of a rest is in `src/` (INV-7); `tools/floor-layout.ts` holds the floor's eight,
`RESTS`, written after the areas with the ids 116 to 123.

Flipped and rotated tiles, more than one tileset per map, embedded tilesets, encoded or
compressed data and infinite maps stay rejected until a feature needs one and lifts its rule. A
tileset tile with an `animation` is rejected too: the game has no animated tiles, and a still
image in place of one would be a silent lie.

## 7. Assets and the generators

`tools/placeholder-tiles.ts` draws the placeholder tileset from code — flat colours and simple
two-colour patterns from Endesga 32 — and returns the pixels and the matching `.tsj` as data.

The shadow tileset (feature 0023), `public/tiles/placeholder-shadow.png`, is the tileset's sheet
in the greys of a region under shadow: 256x48, the same tiles at the same places, the same
transparent pixels. `renderShadowTiles` turns every opaque pixel of `renderTiles` into its grey,

step darker. `npm run gen-tiles` writes it beside the tileset; it is committed, deterministic and
tested against the generator pixel by pixel, and it never uses `#68386c`. It has no `.tsj` of its
own: it is drawn with the tileset's ids, and `main.ts` finds it beside the tileset's image by the
name with `-shadow` before `.png`. A hand-drawn tileset of the art pass brings its shadow sheet
under the same rule.

Every `.tsj` under `public/tiles/` is checked against its PNG: `imagewidth` and `imageheight`
equal the PNG's size, `tilewidth` and `tileheight` are 16, `columns` equals `imagewidth / 16`, and
`tilecount` equals `columns × imageheight / 16`.

When the art pass replaces a tileset by hand (`visual.md` §5), that tileset is removed from the
generator's list and the hand-drawn PNG takes its place under the same name and ids. Nothing in
`src/` changes.

`tools/placeholder-player.ts` draws the placeholder figure the same way, from code in six
Endesga 32 colours, and returns the pixels of its sprite sheet as data; it is pure.
`tools/gen-sprites.ts`, run with `npm run gen-sprites`, writes them as `public/sprites/player.png`.
The sheet is 320x24: twenty 16x24 frames in one row. Frame index = facing × 5 + phase, facings in
the order down (0–4), up (5–9), left (10–14), right (15–19); phase 0 is standing, phases 1–4 are
the walk cycle in order. Facing right is facing left mirrored.

The sheet is not a tileset: it lives under `public/sprites/`, has no `.tsj`, and the
multiple-of-16 rule applies to `public/tiles/` only. The palette, alpha and reserved-colour rules
apply to it as to every PNG under `public/` (INV-6). Like the tiles, the sheet is committed and
its generator is deterministic; a unit test fails when the committed PNG and the generator
disagree. When the art pass draws the figure by hand (`visual.md` §5), the hand-drawn PNG
replaces `public/sprites/player.png` under the same name and frame order. Nothing in `src/`
changes.

`tools/placeholder-lamp.ts` draws the lamp sheet the same way and returns its pixels as data:
`public/sprites/lamp.png`, 192x48, four 48x48 frames in one row in facing order down, up, left,
right, each a checkerboard of `#fee761` cut to the cone from the frame's apex pixel, drawn from
`inCone` of `game/lamp` so the picture is the rule. `npm run gen-sprites` writes both sheets; both
are committed, the generator is deterministic, and a unit test fails when the committed file and
the generator disagree — for the lamp it re-derives every pixel from `inCone` and counts 576
opaque pixels per frame. When the art pass draws the cone by hand (`visual.md` §5), the
hand-drawn PNG replaces the file under the same name, frame order and apex pixels, and the
generator and its tests go; the frame size and the apex pixels stay, because the renderer places
the frame by them.

The same file draws the wider cone's sheet (feature 0029): `public/sprites/lamp-wide.png`,
288x72, four 72x72 frames in the same order, each the checkerboard cut to the cone of spread
3/4 from the frame's apex pixel (`WIDE_FRAME_APEX`), drawn from `inCone` with `WIDE_SPREAD`.
The cone is 48 px long, so 24 px of each frame along the facing are empty. A unit test fails
when the committed file and the generator disagree, re-derives every pixel from `inCone` and
counts 852 opaque pixels per frame.

`tools/placeholder-snippet.ts` draws the snippet sheet (feature 0008) and returns its pixels as

## 8. Text and languages

`text/` holds the language table (feature 0016): one object per language, `de` in `de.ts` and
`en` in `en.ts`. `de` is written first and defines the keys; `en` is typed by the keys of `de`,
so a missing or an extra key does not compile. A key is a dotted name, `<kind>.<what>`, in
lower-case letters, digits and hyphens (digits since feature 0017). `text.ts` exports `LANGUAGES` (`de`, then `en`), the type `Language`,
`DEFAULT_LANGUAGE` (`de`), `TABLE`, and `TextKey`, a branded string type: the only way to get one
is `textKey(id)`, which accepts the ids of the table and nothing else, so a misspelt key does not
compile either. At run time a key is its id, a plain string, so a state that holds one stays
plain data. `textOf(key, language)` returns the string. `game/` and `render/` pass keys; only
`render/draw-text` turns a key into characters, and it accepts a `TextKey` and a language,
nothing else, so a literal string in the game does not compile (INV-4). The strings of the
tables are the only text a player can read in `src/`.

The notice is the first text: `game/notice` derives from the state, in every frame, which notice
it shows, if any. While the lamp is on and a standing shadow wall has `lit` true and `light` 0 —
a wall in the light that holds (0014) — it is `notice.shadow-too-dense`; otherwise, for two
seconds from the step that solved a case, `notice.case-solved` (0024); otherwise, for the two
seconds after those (`UPGRADE_NOTICE_STEPS`, 120), the line of the upgrade the case earned, if
the player has it — `notice.upgrade-wide-cone` (0029, `NOTICE_UPGRADE`); otherwise, for two
seconds from the last find of a trace, `notice.trace-found` (0022); otherwise, for two seconds
(`REST_NOTICE_STEPS`, 120) from a press at a rest, the line of its result — `notice.rest`,
`notice.rest-full` or `notice.rest-shadow` (0027, `NOTICE_REST`); otherwise, while the lamp
was never on, `notice.lamp`, the line that names its key (0026) — in a new game from its first
step (0039), drawn once the call is closed; it has no time and does not come back; otherwise
there is none.
The state has no field for it (INV-1). A notice stays one line.

The language is a setting of the player (feature 0019), not a fact of the game. `main.ts` holds
it: `DEFAULT_LANGUAGE` on every load — the browser's language is not read and nothing is stored
—, changed by `nextLanguage(language)`, the language after it in `LANGUAGES` and the first after
the last. It is not in the state, a saved game does not carry it, and `src/game/` does not know
it: a test fails on the type `Language` under `src/game/`. `window.shadowAi` carries the language
of the last render beside the state, for the smoke test.

A string of the table may hold line breaks; each starts a new line (feature 0017). A page of
dialogue is one key of the kind `talk`, with at most three lines of at most 47 characters and no
empty line; the break is written in the table, and the game computes none, so a translation
decides its own breaks. A test holds every page of both languages to that rule; the glyph test
skips the line breaks. The cast (`game/cast`) names its pages by key, and so does `COLLEAGUES`,
the colleagues' lines by the name of their route (feature 0018).

The journal (feature 0020) adds two kinds. `journal.tab-<tab>` is a tab's title, one line of at
most 16 characters; `journal.no-<tab>` the line a tab shows while it has nothing, one line of at
most 47. `creature.<species>-name` is a species' displayed name, one line of at most 24
characters; `creature.<species>-text` its journal text, two to four lines of at most 47 and no
empty line. `game/journal` names them by key in `TAB_TEXT` and `ENTRY_TEXT`, and a test holds
every key of the two kinds to the rule of its kind in both languages.

The traces (feature 0022) add the kind `region`: `region.<id>` is a region's displayed name,
one line of at most 24 characters, and every region with a trace on the floor has one
(`REGION_NAME` in `game/journal`). `journal.traces` and `journal.of`, the words of a case's line
(`CASE_TEXT`), are one line of at most 16 each. The rule test covers both. A number is no text
of a language: `render/draw-text` draws it with `drawNumber`, which takes a number and nothing
else, and it needs no key; `drawText` still takes a `TextKey` only.

The help (feature 0021) adds one kind. `help.key-<what>` is the name of a key, one line of
at most 17 characters; `help.do-<what>` what it does, one line of at most 16; and `help.label`
(feature 0031) the words of the help label, one line of at most 8. The key `notice.help`, the
start hint of 0021, is gone with feature 0031. `HELP_ROWS` in
`render/draw-help` names them by key, seven rows in the order walk, lamp, talk, journal,
language, fullscreen, help; a test holds every key of the kind to the rule of its kind in both
languages. The letter of a key is a string of the table like every other text, also where it is
the same in both languages.

The cases (feature 0024) add two kinds. `case.<region>-outcome` is a solved case's line on the
cases tab, one line of at most 47 characters; `policy.<region>` is a case's policy block, two
to four lines of at most 47 and no empty line. The source's answer and what it says afterwards
are pages of the kind `talk`, `talk.<region>-source-<n>` and `talk.<region>-after`, under the
rule of a page; `notice.case-solved` is a notice. `game/cases` names them by key in `CASES`,
and the rule test covers both new kinds in both languages. The case of Marketing (feature
0028) adds six keys — `region.marketing` and five of these — and no kind. The case of Support
(feature 0037) adds nine: `region.support`, five of these, and the three hints of the intern.

The story (feature 0026) adds the kind `story`: `story.<scene>-<n>` is a page of a scene, under
the rule of a page of talk — at most three lines of at most 47 characters, none empty — and the
test of that rule covers both kinds. `game/story` names the pages by key in `SCENES`. The close
(feature 0034) adds three pages of the kind, `story.close-1` to `story.close-3`; none of them
holds a number.
`notice.lamp` is a notice. Feature 0039 took two keys out of both tables:
`talk.officer-meeting-2`, the page that handed the lamp over, and `talk.intern-lamp`, the hint
that sent the player for it.

## 10. Dependency policy

Runtime dependencies: none, until a feature needs one and an ADR says which and why. Development
dependencies are the tools above and nothing that drags a framework in through the back door. A
new dependency is a line in the pull request description that says what it does and why it is
not twenty lines of our own code.
